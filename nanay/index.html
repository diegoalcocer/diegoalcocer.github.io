Nanay

During the first months of the pandemic, we created Nanay: a web platform that matched people asking for help with volunteers who could safely deliver it.

We designed every flow for clarity under pressure: categorized requests, verifiable identities, and transparent follow-ups, so everyone involved could see how each case was going.

I co-founded Nanay and led its technology. I also organized the volunteer, social media and marketing teams, and we used data to measure our impact.

Meaning

Why we called it Nanay

In Quichua, Nanay means pain. In other languages, it evokes a caring mother. We embraced both ideas: naming a platform that listens to each story and responds with dignity.

Operations

How it ran

Behind the app were volunteers, donors and on-call coordinators. We verified helpers, routed requests by proximity and urgency, and kept every case visible from start to finish.

The impact

1,000+ volunteers signed up to help.

Brought essentials to 250+ families: food, medicine, and care.

Delivered holiday kits to 90+ families through community donations.
