OAS · 2019 – 2025

Gaia

Replacing a legal case system while people kept using it.

Senior engineer and project manager

Gaia is the case management and document workflow system for one of the OAS's legal bodies. Staff use it every day to open cases, move them through procedural steps, generate and sign documents, and archive them. It replaced two older systems. I led it from design to delivery for six years.

How I ran it

I designed the workflow engine so that every step leaves a record: it checks who may take each step, logs who did what and when, and rolls back cleanly if something fails. New features shipped behind switches, so we could test them, roll them out gradually or turn them off without redeploying a system people relied on daily.

Usage data and user feedback set the priorities: the slowest modules first, then simpler screens. Before building, we reviewed designs with real users, and a monthly bulletin kept several departments informed about what had changed, what was coming and what the data showed.

What changed

Archiving and a fifth of all document signings were automated, manual deliveries fell by 90%, and page performance scores rose from about 40 to over 90. When I left, the team had the documentation, tests and a written handover, so the system didn't depend on me.

What I took from it

Keep the backbone simple, and make the work visible. Delivery that nobody hears about loses much of its value.
